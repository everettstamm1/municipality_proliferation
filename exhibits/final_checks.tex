\section{Disincorporation Idea}

Chris Goodman Data allows us to measure year of incorporation for municipalities conditional on it's survival to the present. The Census of governments gives us the count of municipalities in 5 year windows. We can back out the number of amalgamated or disincorporated municipalities from the a stock-flow identity

\begin{align*}
	\Delta \text{Munis}_{1940-70} = \text{New Munis}_{1940-70} - \text{Dead Munis}_{1940-70}
\end{align*}

We have that $\Delta \text{Munis}_{1940-1970}$ is given by the CoG. We also have that $\text{New Munis}_{1940-1970}$ is given by the C. Goodman Data. So "Dead" munis, either disincorporations or amalgamations, can be approximated by the difference in the outcomes from the two data sources
\begin{align*}
	\text{Dead Munis}_{1940-70} = \Delta \text{Munis}_{1940-70}^{\text{CGoodman}} -  \Delta \text{Munis}_{1940-70}^{\text{CoG}}
\end{align*}
Note this probably works best for the 2010 long-run outcomes as the Chris Goodman data is conditional on survival to the present, meaning it would undercount any instances where a city is incorporated between 1940-70 but dies between 1970-present.
